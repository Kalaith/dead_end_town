# Player experience and presentation

## Opening sequence

The timings below are illustrative playtest targets, not a strict clock or a confirmed tutorial script.

| Time | Experience | What it teaches |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 minutes | Find a bat and repel a small approach at the starting home | Move, attack, recognise a damaged entrance |
| 5–10 | Repair personally; leave the starting companion guarding | Shared construction and trusted defence |
| 10–15 | Cross into the neighbour's yard and clear it | Continuous travel and squad assistance |
| 15–20 | Claim the garden house, connect its gate, start a garden order | Cleared versus secured; physical work |
| 20–25 | Observe an approach while away from the original house | Alert, guard response, choice to assist |
| 25–30 | Choose between the library route and garage route | Rescue versus equipment/facility rewards |

Paige's rescue follows once the basic controls are understood. It should show her overwhelmed by a manageable threat, then give her useful work immediately after rescue. Her later armour milestone echoes that introduction without requiring a scripted helplessness phase.

## Essential interfaces

**World HUD:** health, equipped tool or weapon, relevant ammunition, squad orders, and concise local alerts. Show construction progress at the work site.

**Settlement overview:** connected properties, exposed edges, guard posts, unsafe routes, and facility status. Selecting a weak edge focuses the camera or marks a route; the overview should not imply walls exist where there are only plans.

**Survivor panel:** assignment, current activity and location, equipment, recovery, and emergency behaviour. One clear reason explains why a job or order cannot proceed.

**Work board:** approved jobs, priority, progress, assigned worker, resource commitment, and block reason. Keep routine work automatic.

**Discovery notification:** what was found, what capability it enables, and what further facility or material requirement remains. Avoid displaying an unlock as usable when the workshop is still derelict.

## Visible town evolution

Abandoned properties show damaged openings, overgrowth, and occupants. Secured properties gain repaired access and a modest community marker. Development adds the structures actually built: crops, storage, workbenches, reinforced entrances. Integration opens physical walkways and shared courtyards.

These are descriptions of world changes, not four mandatory upgrade levels. Damage remains visible until repaired. Internal fences stay until a work order changes them. Workers walk to tasks; defenders visibly fight. Do not substitute an animated progress icon for every important world action.

## Character and equipment readability

Keep strong silhouettes and stable identity cues across loadouts. Shield, firearm, and heavy armour must be readable at gameplay scale. Layered equipment sprites are a proposed implementation, not an art requirement until the direction and animation budget are chosen.

## Audio and accessibility

Differentiate construction, melee impacts, gunfire, approach warnings, and breaches. Every critical alert also needs text and a map marker. Do not rely on colour alone for property states or guard coverage. Offer readable text scaling, remappable controls, and adjustable repeated alert sounds.

Proposed single-player default: pause the whole world in pause/settings screens, keep the world running during brief HUD actions, and evaluate a planning pause for construction and orders. The exact pause policy is open and must be consistent across combat, jobs, production, and distant simulation.

## Presentation review

Watch a player who has not read these docs. They should be able to explain why one house is safe, why another is merely cleared, what the home guard is doing, and what their latest discovery enables. If they cannot, improve world feedback before adding more tutorials.
